Agents instead of armies

Large system changes have always been staffed like construction projects. They do not have to be.

The standard way to change an enterprise system is to hire a lot of people. Consultants to read the old system, analysts to sample the data, testers to check the samples. The cost scales with headcount and the quality scales with how much of the data anyone had time to look at.

Read everything, not samples

Agents change the shape of that work. An agent does not sample. It reads every table, every document and every posting, and it does so again on every run. The coverage that used to be a budget decision becomes the default.

But reading is not the hard part, and neither is writing the transformation. The hard part is judgment: the handful of decisions in every programme that only someone who knows the business can make. That part should stay with people.

People decide, agents prove

So Ygdra is built around a simple division of labour. Agents do the reading, the transformation and the reconciliation, and they surface every judgment call with the evidence behind it. People make the decisions. Every decision is recorded, reused, and carried into the evidence pack.

The result is a smaller team that sees more of the data than a large one ever could, and a programme whose reasoning is on the record rather than in the heads of people who will roll off it.

Built in Stockholm

We started Ygdra in Stockholm because European enterprises carry some of the most demanding audit and data-residency expectations in the world. If the approach works under that scrutiny, it works anywhere.
