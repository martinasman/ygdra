Perspective · September 2026

Confidence is not evidence

Why we never quote an accuracy score, and what we hand over instead.

Every AI vendor selling into finance eventually gets asked the same question: how accurate is it? The honest answer, for any system doing real work on a real company’s books, is that the question is the wrong one.

Accuracy is a claim. Conservation is a fact.

A migration is full of judgment calls. Which account a custom field belongs to. Whether a cleansing rule changes what a figure means. Whether a period 13 posting belongs in the opening balance. None of these have an answer you can score against, so any percentage attached to them is a number someone chose to believe.

What can be proven is simpler and stronger. Every figure in the source ledger is accounted for in the target. The books tie out to the line, in every currency. Nothing was silently dropped and nothing was invented. Where something does not balance, it is named: the account, the document, the amount.

Show the work

That is why every answer in Ygdra carries the work that produced it. The query that was run. The rows that were read. The rule that was applied and the person who approved it. An agent that cannot show its work does not get to call the work done.

The result is a different kind of conversation with a controller or an auditor. Not “trust us, it is right”, but “here is why it is right, and here is how you can check it yourself.” We think that is the only honest way to put AI in front of the numbers a company reports.

What we hand over

At the end of a Ygdra run, the deliverable is not a score. It is an evidence pack: the reconciliation, the decisions, the approvals and the source rows behind each of them, signed so that anyone can verify nothing changed after the fact. Confidence is something you feel. Evidence is something you can hand to someone else.

Ygdra, Stockholm
